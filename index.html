World Models for Physical AI: Learning to Predict, Plan, and Act in the Real World

Differences in representations, action interfaces, and evaluation protocols obscure when better predictions yield better decisions. This survey reviews data foundations and modelling approaches across robotics, navigation, and autonomous driving, then examines three gaps between what a world model predicts and what a downstream consumer actually gains.

Abstract

Better predictions, unclear decisions

World models, which predict how environments evolve under actions, increasingly support simulation, policy learning, planning, and evaluation in physical AI. However, differences in representations, action interfaces, and evaluation protocols obscure when better predictions yield better decisions.

Drawing on experiments and counterexamples, we examine gaps between recursive accuracy and planning utility, evaluator agreement and policy-training outcomes, and predictive information and execution-time correction. We organise evidence across five uses, identify unresolved attribution questions, and discuss requirements for reliable, action-grounded world modelling.

Part I — §1 Foundations and Scope

Three coordinates, then the evidence they demand

The manuscript places a world model at three descriptive coordinates before assessing it. The fourth node is the one the survey adds: each coordinate fixes a different set of admissible evidence.

1.2The analytical framework

Prediction target Pixels or video · latent state · objects and geometry · physical and multimodal variables
Action query Passive · supplied candidates · goal-conditioned · joint world–action
Prediction consumer Simulation · policy learning · policy evaluation · planning · correction
Evidence required A subset of L1–L6, selected by the consumer — never a single score

Read left to right: the same architecture can sit at different coordinates. V-JEPA 2 answers supplied candidate actions; UniPi generates a goal-conditioned future and decodes it with inverse dynamics; GNS advances a supplied physical state without accepting new commands; Dreamer exposes no executable action query at all. Output modality alone never identifies the coordinate.

1.3Working definitions

A world model is a learned predictor of environmental evolution: future observations, latent states, object relations, physical quantities, or action consequences. An action-conditioned model predicts consequences under specified actions or interventions; a passive model need not expose executable-action queries. Prediction, policy, and control are computational roles, not necessarily separate networks — action generation alone does not establish consequence prediction.

Part II — §2 Data, Representations, and Predictive Models

Which link of the chain each resource covers

Predicting observed motion and predicting a robot command's consequence are different supervision problems. The manuscript's test is whether a resource pairs a supplied command with a measured environmental response — and most do not.

Commanded action Supplied by the policy, with an explicit frame, duration and control mode
Realized motion L3 · does this command produce this robot or agent motion?
Environment response L4 · does that motion displace the object or change contact?
New observation Fed back as the next input, closing the loop

Coverage, per resource. DROID and Open X-Embodiment align the first two links and assess data mixtures. PhysInOne labels links two to four with depth, trajectories and materials, but ships no robot-command trajectories. IntPhys 2, Physion and PHYRE test event judgement and contact at the third node only. No resource in the Core Evidence Set pairs a commanded action with its measured environmental response — the manuscript names that paired-state intervention as a proposed, unexecuted test.

2.1The intervention gap in data

Synchronized corpora establish the command–observation link without erasing control conventions. A shared storage format does not itself align executable command meanings: action frame, duration, control mode, observation frequency, and embodiment remain part of the supervision. Pooling more trajectories can therefore change which transition a training pair describes rather than simply broadening coverage.

2.2Video as feedback, not only forecast

Returning generated observations to an agent changes the consumer even when the predictor is unchanged. In Interactive World Simulator, teleoperators supply commands to collect synthetic demonstrations, whereas policy evaluation feeds generated observations back to a policy. The first interface creates training examples; the second lets the tested policy choose its own subsequent inputs. Neither reduces to the fidelity of an independently sampled video.

2.3Latent and structured representations

A representation matters through the distinctions its consumer needs: derivatives for an integrator, attainable geometric change for inverse dynamics, or anticipated deformation for contact control. A compact encoder is not yet a queryable transition. With supplied physical state, graphs organise a numerical update rather than discover objects — MeshGraphNets combines mesh and spatial-contact connectivity and integrates node outputs, optionally remeshing.

2.4Generalization across scenes and embodiments

Three bridges coexist — transferring experience, adapting executable actions, and preserving selected outputs — but their evidence concerns different changes rather than one generalization score. Sim-to-real WAM adapts Cosmos Policy with roughly 800 simulated demonstrations per task and reports 35% mean zero-shot success across four Franka tasks. That measures the complete transfer recipe, not an isolated contribution of world-model prediction.

Part III — §3 Learning, Simulation, and Decision Making

Each consumer is scored by a different quantity

This is the survey's mechanism. A result in one column cannot be carried into another, and the three gaps stated in the abstract are exactly mismatches between a claimed column and a measured one.

Simulation Rollout error · GNS: recursion ↑ while one-step ↓
Policy learning Trained return · Fast-WAM −8.0 / −4.1 pp without video co-training
Evaluation Agreement with independent outcomes · DexTouch r = .844 / .646
Planning Task success under a protocol · Yuan 68.9 / 79.2%; ContactWorld 32.3 vs 39.5%
Correction Detect → revise → recover · TacPAC 47 → 64%, Bottle 40 / 40%

Numbers are within-study anchors, not a ranking. Fast-WAM's two figures are its own RoboTwin and LIBERO deltas; ContactWorld's pair is point-cloud versus full tactile on the same 12-task protocol. Comparing across nodes would compare unlike endpoints — which is the error the survey is arguing against.

3.1Planning with predicted consequences

Consequence planning must both propose a useful motion and recognise its value before committing. PETS propagates sampled trajectories through probabilistic dynamics ensembles and uses CEM; PlaNet runs CEM on predicted latent states and rewards without rendering images for scoring; TD-MPC2 guides latent search with a learned policy prior and a terminal value. Ensemble uncertainty, a visual belief, and policy/value guidance change different parts of the decision, not merely alternative forecast formats.

3.2Driving and navigation as an interface test

Three interfaces appear: searching supplied candidates, conditioning action generation on predicted context, and refreshing decisions from feedback. In the first, prediction changes a ranking; in the second it supplies features or training targets; in the third the next computation is anchored either to generated history or to the executed world. These distinctions determine which ablation explains a reported gain.

3.3Policy learning with imagined experience

What the optimizer consumes determines how imagination changes learning. Dreamer differentiates imagined returns through fixed latent dynamics; MBPO supplies short predicted transitions to SAC updates; DreamerV3 uses normalized REINFORCE. MBPO's Hopper ablations retain much of the benefit with one-step branches, degrade with long branches, and outperform extra no-model SAC updates — so transition accumulation, return truncation, and state information are three different weaknesses.

3.4Memory, failure detection, and recovery

Memory preserves context; recovery changes what happens after a failure. A history-conditioned forecast can support simulation, and a history-conditioned actor can maintain task progress, without either constructing a response to an impending failure. Mem-World's policy-evaluation experiment reports imagined–real success correlation of r = 0.97 — evaluator agreement, not an online recovery endpoint.

3.5Visual plans and joint world–action models

Predictive supervision and runtime future information answer different questions. Fast-WAM's base action tokens read features of the current observation, not future-video tokens, and removing video co-training leaves architecture and inference unchanged while costing the 8.0 and 4.1 points cited above. Executing only decoded actions does not remove future tokens from sampling, so information available during denoising and outputs retained afterwards are two descriptions of one system.

3.6Reasoner-assisted revision: a boundary comparator

Revision by a reasoner changes a proposal by interpreting observations and task constraints; it need not evaluate a predicted trajectory. Astra reports 48% hybrid versus 26% direct success on ten RoboDojo tasks. The manuscript treats this as a comparator that sharpens what counts as a prediction consumer, rather than as a sixth consumer to add to the list.

Part IV — §4 Evaluation, Safety, and Reliability

Six checks, and the boundary where each one fails

The levels are claim-dependent checks, not six mandatory gates. The caption below records what the manuscript reports at the two boundaries that are most often crossed silently.

4.1Prediction fidelity and decision utility

L1Perceptual fidelity · does the future look coherent?
L2State and physical fidelity · dynamics, geometry, contact
L3Action realization · command → motion
L4Interaction grounding · motion → world response
L5Planning and policy utility · choice or learning
L6Operational reliability · failure, timing, recovery

The arrows trace how a claim travels, not a gate sequence. AVID already separates L1 from L3 inside one evaluation: RT-1 frame metrics score generated appearance, while Action Error Ratio compares generated and real video through a video-to-action regressor — a motion proxy, not robot execution. GNS shows L2 recursion can trade against one-step L2. ContactWorld shows L2 error growth need not order L5 success: 32.3% point clouds, 39.5% full tactile, 38.6% visual-only cost, 36.5% width-matched, across variants of one protocol.

4.2Uncertainty, constraints, and runtime guardrails

Safety mechanisms intervene at different operations: exploration, policy optimisation, command acceptance, or alarm generation. Confidence-bounded Lyapunov learning restricts exploration to an invariant region; predictive safety filtering tests a backup trajectory; a control-barrier-function QP constrains the instantaneous derivative. Each requires a valid state and uncertainty model plus, respectively, a stabilising policy, a feasible terminal backup, or an admissible safe input.

A visual forecast supplies none of these automatically, and an alarm is not a completed intervention.

4.3Operational reliability and attribution

FutureRTC isolates action-index alignment under an oracle context: the first generated action yields 96.6% success, while starting at index k = 20 after the same delay yields 0.9%. This is an oracle-context diagnostic, not evidence that the model can access that future observation when it generates the action. Intervention can itself cause failure: VLA-FixBench's automatic judgers achieve 0–25% completion versus 30% policy-only and 65% human intervention on two physical Franka tasks.

Synthesis

What the evidence supports, and what is still unrun

Two tables carry the empirical claim. They are reproduced as placeholders here and will be replaced with screenshots of the original tables.

Why no other tables. The per-model interface matrix in the manuscript marks, for each system, whether it answers supplied actions, generates goal-conditioned futures, or exposes explicit structure. Reproducing those marks faithfully needs the original column geometry, so the distinction is described in prose and in the Part I chain instead. The evaluation and safety audits are reference tables for readers comparing protocols, not claims that belong on a summary page.

4.4Three validation choices the evidence supports

GNS's recursive robustness cannot replace ContactWorld's goal-scored planning endpoint; DexTouch-WM's evaluator ranking does not select a training-data generator independently of the learner, as IWS's mixtures also illustrate; and FutureRTC's action-index alignment differs from TacPAC's access to a predicted tactile reference. Existing surveys establish the broader evaluation and attribution principles; the contribution here is to connect specific controls and adverse outcomes to these distinct choices.

§5 Open problems

What this survey cannot settle

Each item is a testable gap, in the form the survey argues future work should adopt.

01

Unobserved candidates

Rejected candidates' physical outcomes are unobserved. Until a protocol branches reproducible failure states while fixing candidate set, continuation policy, horizon and repeats, selection regret cannot be estimated from executed runs.

02

Learner-dependent utility

Evaluator agreement does not select a training-data generator independently of the learner. A single correlation statistic cannot stand in for the downstream recipe.

03

Mixed runtime endpoints

Correction access, action-index alignment, and update timing are distinct operations. No reported study collapses them into a single runtime-efficiency claim.

04

Guarantees without interfaces

Formal certificates assume a valid state and uncertainty model plus a stabilising policy, a feasible backup, or an admissible safe input. A visual forecast provides none of these.

05

Intervention harms

Automatic repair pipelines scored below policy-only baselines on physical tasks. Reliability requires measuring the consequence of an intervention, not only its timing.

06

Sampling bias

The Core Evidence Set is mechanism-focused, not a representative sample. The prevalence of benefits and failures cannot be inferred from it, and inclusion rationales were recorded retrospectively.
